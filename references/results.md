# Results

## Use this reference when

The user is organizing, drafting, interpreting, or revising Results, deciding between separate and combined Results/Discussion sections, or integrating figures and tables into result reporting.

## Section arrangements

The source presents four possible arrangements:

1. Results; Discussion; Conclusion.
2. Results & Discussion; Conclusion.
3. Results; Discussion.
4. Results; Discussion and conclusions.

The source highlights the first two in one visual but does not invalidate the others. Select the arrangement from the target journal and paper design.

## Results versus Discussion

In a separate Results section, foreground what was observed or found and avoid excessive interpretation. Discussion addresses why a finding may have occurred, what it means, how it compares with prior work, and what it implies. In a combined section, reporting and interpretation may be interleaved, but their functions should remain distinguishable.

The source itself contains a boundary ambiguity: it advises limiting interpretation in a separate Results section but also lists “commenting on results” as an optional Results move. Preserve that distinction rather than treating either statement as an absolute rule.

## Rhetorical moves

| Move | Function |
|---|---|
| 1. Introductory information | Research question, purpose, method, or a results-section overview. |
| 2. Present results | In prose, visuals, or both. |
| 3. Comment on results (optional) | Interpretation, evaluation, explanation, or comparison; especially relevant in a combined section. |
| 4. Summarize results | Consolidate the reported findings. |

For each result paragraph, check which move it performs and whether the amount of interpretation fits the section arrangement.

## Tense

- Past simple: what the researchers did and found.
- Present simple: an always-true situation, locating data in a figure/table, or comparing results with other studies.

Use the tense that reflects the rhetorical function and time relation; do not mechanically change every result to one tense.

## Reporting findings

The source's sentence patterns include `Table 1 shows ...`, `Figure 1 illustrates ...`, `Our results showed ...`, and `These results indicate/suggest ...`. Treat `showed`, `indicate`, and `suggest` as different stance choices: the wording must match what the data support.
