# Conclusion(s)

## Use this reference when

The user is writing or revising a Conclusion, Summary, Summary and Conclusions, Concluding Remarks, Final Remarks, or Conclusion and Future Work section, or needs to distinguish the final section from the Abstract or Discussion.

## Status and functions

The source describes Conclusions as optional but increasingly important. Possible labels include `Conclusion`, `Summary`, `Summary and conclusions`, `Concluding remarks`, `Final remarks`, and `Conclusion and future work`.

The section can:

1. provide a generalized review and evaluation of the study;
2. move from a concise summary of principal findings toward theoretical or abstract comment;
3. state implications or applications.

## Move structure

| Move | Sub-moves |
|---|---|
| 1. Summarize the present research | Motivation; objective; major/overall findings. |
| 2. Evaluate the present research positively | Merits of the study. |
| 3. Discuss limitations | Limitation; justification or explanation. |
| 4. Provide a deduction | Implications; future research. |

The source does not require every move in every Conclusion. Use only the moves needed by the paper and the target journal.

## Conclusion versus Abstract and Introduction

The source describes the Abstract as an attention-attracting “advertisement” and the Conclusion as a reminder of the paper's salient points, often with implications, recommendations, or future research. A Conclusion should not simply duplicate the Abstract and Introduction; it should leave a clear high-impact message while preserving the actual evidence level.

Source patterns such as `The results of this study indicate that ...`, `These findings suggest ...`, and `Notwithstanding these limitations, the study suggests ...` are optional realizations of functions, not required formulas.

## Placement decision

If the paper combines Discussion and Conclusion, integrate the functions without labeling a short summary as a separate Conclusion unless the journal permits it. If the paper has a separate Conclusion, decide explicitly whether limitations, contributions, and future research belong there or in Discussion; do not repeat them without purpose.
